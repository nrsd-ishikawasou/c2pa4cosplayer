## 2. Items

### L-1“Removal or alteration of rights management information (Japan)” Japan: Rights management information

- Definition (Copyright Act Article 2(1)(xxii); item (xxi) is technological measures restricting use): information on copyrights and the like that falls under any of (a) information identifying the work, the copyright holder, or other matters specified by Cabinet Order, (b) information on the manner and conditions of use when use is permitted, or (c) information that makes it possible to identify the matters of (a) or (b) by collating with other information, and that is recorded on a recording medium together with the work or transmitted together with it by electromagnetic means. However, excluding information “not used for managing copyrights and the like (limited to management by computer), such as understanding the use of works or processing affairs related to permitting their use”. [J1]
- Deemed infringement (Article 113(8)): ① intentionally adding false information as rights management information; ② intentionally removing or altering rights management information (“except where it is due to technical constraints accompanying the conversion of the method of recording or transmission, or where it is otherwise found unavoidable in light of the purpose and manner of the use of the work or performance”); ③ knowingly distributing or publicly transmitting copies on which these have been done. [J1]
- Penalties (Article 120-2(v)): anyone who performs the acts of Article 113(8) for commercial purposes is subject to imprisonment of up to 3 years, a fine of up to JPY 3 million, or both. This offense is prosecuted only on complaint (Article 123(1)). [J1]
- The “information that makes identification possible by collation” of (c) is one basis for interpreting the identification number (placed in the watermark and manifest, and collated with the work data on the User's device to identify the work and the Rights Holder; Basic Design Chapter 3, 7.1 “Identification number”) as rights management information (requires confirmation by experts).
- Consequences for the design
  - For the C2PA manifest and the watermark's identifier to be “rights management information”, they must contain information on copyrights and the like and be used for management by computer (collation, processing of permissions). The System uses them for collation, so there is room to interpret them as such (requires confirmation by experts). The design that records the Rights Holder and terms of use in the manifest (Chapter 3 “Signing”) supports this interpretation.
  - Removal “due to technical constraints accompanying the conversion of the method” is excluded. Loss through social media re-encoding, or loss when purchasers convert the format, may not be intentional removal. Design Plan D-7-6“A missing signature is treated as something that can be concluded to be intentional removal by the recipient” is therefore limited under Japanese law. China (L-2“Removal or alteration of rights management information (China)”) and the United States (L-3“Removal or alteration of rights management information (US)”) have limitations to the same effect. Declared as a gap in Outline Design Document 13-4 “Declaration of gaps”.

### L-2“Removal or alteration of rights management information (China)” China: Rights management information

- Copyright Law (2020 amendment) Article 51 (prohibition of removing and altering rights management information), Article 53 (infringing acts), Article 54 (damages; punitive damages of one to five times for intentional infringement; statutory damages up to CNY 5 million). From the source in Draft Project Proposal [16]. [C1]
- Article 51(1) prohibits “intentionally” deleting or altering rights management information without the rights holder's permission, “由于技术上的原因无法避免的除外” (except where unavoidable for technical reasons). Loss through format conversion and recompression may fall under this exception. [C1]

### L-3“Removal or alteration of rights management information (US)” United States: Copyright management information

- 17 U.S.C. §1202 (integrity of copyright management information). Section 1203(c)(3)(B): statutory damages of not less than USD 2,500 or more than USD 25,000 may be elected per violation of Section 1202. [U1] [U2]
- Section 1202(b) prohibits, without the authority of the copyright owner or the law, “intentionally” removing or altering copyright management information and the like, knowing (or, with respect to civil remedies under Section 1203, having reasonable grounds to know) that it will induce, enable, facilitate, or conceal an infringement. There is a double requirement of intent and knowledge of the connection to infringement, and loss through format conversion and recompression usually does not fall under it. [U1]

### L-4“Protection of rights management information (treaty) (international)” International: WIPO Copyright Treaty Article 12

- Contracting parties are obliged to provide legal remedies against acts such as removing or altering electronic rights management information without authority. [Design Plan [27]]

### L-5“Portrait rights and publicity rights (Japan)” Japan: Portrait rights, publicity rights

- Portrait rights and publicity rights are based on case law. The Supreme Court judgment of February 2, 2012 recognized the right to exclusively use the customer-attracting power of names, portraits, and the like (publicity right) as derived from personality rights, and held that it is infringed where the purpose is “solely to use the customer-attracting power of the portrait, etc.”, such as ① using the portrait itself as a product to be appreciated independently, ② attaching the portrait to products to differentiate them, or ③ using the portrait as advertising for products. [J2]
- On portrait rights (the personal interest in not being photographed or published without good reason, as distinct from publicity rights), the Supreme Court judgment of November 10, 2005 (Minshu Vol. 59 No. 9 p. 2428, 2003 (Ju) No. 281) held that a person has a personal interest in not having their appearance photographed without good reason, and that whether photographing constitutes a tort is determined by whether the infringement of the personal interest “exceeds the limit of tolerance in social life”, considering comprehensively the social status of the person photographed, the content of the activity photographed, and the place, purpose, manner, and necessity of the photographing; publishing the photographs is judged likewise. [J25] This judgment concerned publishing photographs taken in court without permission and an illustration. Since most subjects are not celebrities, NRSD thinks that this limit-of-tolerance standard, rather than publicity rights, also applies to the publication of portraits through unauthorized reposting of photographs taken with consent, but whether it applies requires confirmation by experts (reasoning without a source).
- Consequences for the design: sale and advertising use of unauthorized reposts (“commercial use” in Section 8 of the Draft Project Proposal) are close to ② and ③. Portrait requests for non-celebrity subjects follow the standard of whether the limit of tolerance is exceeded (Basic Design Chapter 7, 3.4 “Standing of the complainant”). The guidance (Chapter 7 “Legal Action Guidance”) can show this as one basis for prioritizing commercial use.

### L-7“Takedown request procedures (Japan)” Japan: Takedown requests

- On April 1, 2025, the Provider Liability Limitation Act was reorganized into the “Act on Measures against Infringement of Rights Arising from the Distribution of Information through Specified Telecommunications” (Information Distribution Platform Act). It obliges Large-Scale Specified Telecommunications Service Providers to respond to takedown requests, formulate and publish takedown criteria, and so on. The result of the decision on a request is notified to the requester within the period set by Ministerial Ordinance (7 days). [J3] [J4]
- Provisions (obtained through the e-Gov law search API): Article 20 (designation of Large-Scale Specified Telecommunications Service Providers), Article 21 (notification within 3 months of designation), Article 22 (publication of request methods: electronic means, no excessive burden, the requester can know the time of receipt), Article 23 (investigation without delay), Article 25 (notify, within a period set by Ministerial Ordinance within 14 days of receiving the request, whether measures were taken and, if not, the reason; the Enforcement Regulations set 7 days; when hearing the sender's opinion or the like, notify that fact within the period and notify without delay after the decision), Article 27 (notice to the sender), Article 3(2)(ii) (if the sender is asked for consent and no objection is made within 7 days, the provider that removed it is not liable to the sender). [J3] [J22]
- The duties of investigation under Article 23 and notification under Article 25 apply when the infringed person makes a request “in accordance with the method of paragraph 1 of the preceding Article” (the method the provider published under Article 22(1)). Even for the same provider, requests sent through contact points other than the published method, such as U.S. DMCA report forms, do not carry these duties. [J22]
- The requester's liability for damage caused to the sender by a mistaken request is not provided for in this Act and follows general tort law (Civil Code Article 709) (requires confirmation by experts). [J10]
- Designations (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications press releases): April 30, 2025: Google (YouTube), LY Corporation, Meta (Facebook, Instagram, Threads), TikTok, X. May 30, 2025: Pinterest, CyberAgent (Ameba Blog), Shonan Seibu Home (Bakusai.com), Dwango (Niconico). August 31, 2026: J Squared (Girls Channel), pixiv (pixiv), note, an individual (Sukikirai.com). Each company's request contact point appears in the Ministry's “List of takedown request contact points and takedown criteria notified under Article 21”. [J23]
- Form for copyright requests: “Information Distribution Platform Act Copyright Guidelines”, 3rd edition (May 2025, Copyright WG of the Council for Reviewing Guidelines of the Information Distribution Platform Act). The items of Form A, confirmation of the requester's identity (for paper, name and seal and a copy of an official certificate, etc.; persons overseas may substitute a signature), materials confirming that one is the copyright holder or the like (certificate of registration, a copy of the display of the name, etc. (Copyright Act Article 14), materials publicly available before the request, an appropriately managed database), identification of the infringing information (URL and features), manner of infringement (verbatim copying, etc.), and a statement that permission was not given. [J24]
- The Copyright Guidelines, 3rd edition, limit requesters to copyright holders and the like, and do not cover requests from third parties (note to II 1, III 1). Portrait requests by subjects are not covered by copyright requests. [J24]
- Consequences for the design: the Japanese request model text follows Form A, and the 7-day notification deadline is shown only when the request is made through the contact point of the designated provider's published method. The complainant's standing (copyright holder, subject, agent) is chosen first (Basic Design Chapter 7, DD-7-5“The Japanese model text follows Form A”, 3.3.3 “Japan (request for measures to prevent transmission of infringing information; Form A of the Copyright Guidelines, 3rd edition)”, 3.4 “Standing of the complainant”, 6.3 “Display of procedural deadlines”).

### L-8“Takedown request procedures (China)” China: Content of notices

- Regulations on the Protection of the Right of Communication through Information Network Article 14: a rights holder may, in writing, ask providers of information storage space, search, or link services to remove or disconnect links, on the grounds that a work infringes the right of communication through information network or that rights management electronic information has been deleted or altered. The notice includes ① the rights holder's name, contact details, and address, ② the name and network address of the infringing work, and so on. [C2]
- Article 15: after receiving the notice, the provider immediately removes or disconnects the link and forwards the notice to the service recipient. Article 16: a service recipient who considers it not infringing may request restoration with a written explanation (name, contact details, address, name and location of the work to be restored, and preliminary evidence of non-infringement). Article 17: after receiving the written explanation, the provider must immediately restore the removed work (“应当立即恢复”) and may restore disconnected links (“可以恢复”). At the same time it forwards the explanation to the rights holder. The rights holder may not again notify removal of the same work. Article 24: if the provider mistakenly removes because of the rights holder's notice and causes loss to the service recipient, the rights holder compensates. Article 26: rights management electronic information means information on the work and author, the rights holder, and the conditions of use, and the like. [C2]
- Civil Code (in force 2021) Article 1195: a rights holder may notify a network service provider to take necessary measures such as deletion, blocking, or disconnecting links. The notice includes preliminary evidence constituting infringement and the rights holder's true identity information. The provider forwards the notice to the network user and takes measures. A rights holder whose mistaken notice causes damage to the network user or provider bears tort liability (paragraph 3). Article 1196: a network user may issue a statement of non-infringement (including preliminary evidence and true identity information). The provider forwards the statement to the rights holder and informs them that they may complain or sue. If, within a “reasonable period” after the forwarded statement reaches the rights holder, the provider does not receive notice that the rights holder has complained or sued, the provider promptly ends the measures. [C12]
- E-Commerce Law (in force 2019) Articles 42 and 43: set the same framework for e-commerce platforms, with the period “within fifteen days”. Malicious false notices are compensated at double the loss (Article 42). [C13]
- The relation between the Regulations (Articles 15 to 17: with a written counter explanation, the removed work must be restored immediately) and the Civil Code (the measures end if no complaint or suit is filed within a reasonable period) requires confirmation by experts.
- Consequences for the design: complaints require the User's name and address (true identity information) (item breakdown omission A-21“Complaints passing the User's personal information to the other party”). That the repost is restored unless a complaint or suit is filed within a reasonable period after a counter (15 days for e-commerce platforms) is shown as a procedural deadline (Basic Design Chapter 7, 6.3 “Display of procedural deadlines”). This is stated in advance in the guidance (R-7-2-2“Users can know in advance that complaints may pass their personal information to the other party”). Liability for compensation for mistaken notices (Regulations Article 24, Civil Code Article 1195(3), E-Commerce Law Article 42) is shown before complaints (Basic Design Chapter 7, DD-7-6“Liability for wrongful complaints is shown first”). The definition of Article 26 supports the design of recording the Rights Holder and conditions of use in the C2PA manifest (Basic Design Chapter 3).

### L-9“Takedown request procedures (US)” United States: DMCA notices

- Section 512(c)(3)(A): a notice includes ① the signature of an authorized person, ② identification of the infringed work, ③ identification and location of the material claimed to be infringing, ④ information to contact the complainant (address, phone number, and e-mail if any), ⑤ a statement of good faith belief that it is not authorized, ⑥ a statement that the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that the complainant is authorized. [U3]
- Section 512(g): upon a counter-notification, the provider sends a copy to the complainant, tells them it will restore the material in 10 business days, and, unless notified that the complainant has filed an action, restores it no less than 10 and no more than 14 business days after receiving the counter-notification ((g)(2)(B)(C)). A counter-notification includes a signature, identification of the removed material, a statement under penalty of perjury that it was removed by mistake, contact details, and consent to jurisdiction ((g)(3)). [U3]
- Section 512(f): anyone who knowingly materially misrepresents that material is infringing is liable for damages (including costs and attorneys' fees) incurred by those harmed. [U3]
- Consequences for the design: the complainant's contact information may also reach the other party (the counter-notifier). This is stated in advance in the guidance.
- In the United States, there are court decisions holding that claims for removal of rights management information (Section 1202) do not require registration, while statutory damages for copyright infringement in principle require registration (Section 412). [U5] [U6] [U7] Therefore, removal of watermarks and C2PA may be a strong basis for claims in the United States for Users without registration (requires confirmation by experts).

### L-10“Handling of legal business (unauthorized practice of law) (Japan)” Japan: Unauthorized practice of law (Attorney Act Article 72)

- Requirements: a person who is not an attorney, for the purpose of obtaining remuneration, handling legal services concerning legal cases as a business (unlawful when all requirements are met). [J5]
- Ministry of Justice guidelines (August 2023, “On the relationship between the provision of services supporting contract-related work using AI and the like and Article 72 of the Attorney Act”):
  - “Where a business provides the service without receiving any benefit whatsoever, including usage fees, it usually does not fall under ‘for the purpose of obtaining remuneration’ and does not violate the Article.” However, leading to other paid services, receiving consideration from third parties' paid services, and providing only to those who have paid membership fees and the like may fall under “for the purpose of obtaining remuneration”. [J5]
  - Preparing settlement agreements and the like after a dispute has arisen may be found to involve a “legal case”. [J5]
  - Cases are shown where functions such as selecting from registered model forms and displaying them as they are do not fall under “appraisal ... or other legal services” of the Article. [J5]
- Consequences for the design: complaints about reposts involve a “legal case”. That the System is provided free of charge, does not lead to paid legal services of NRSD or third parties, and limits text to presenting general model forms without stating individual legal opinions is the basis for the line drawn in Chapter 7 (R-7-6-1“It is stated explicitly that this is not legal advice, and the sources of information are stated”). The “attorney in charge” in Section 9 of the Draft Project Proposal is for responding to lawsuits concerning the operation of the System, and must not become a path to handling Users' complaints for a fee.

### L-11“Personal information protection (User information) (Japan)” Japan: Protection of personal information

- When using a cloud provided by a third party located abroad, if the cloud provider is not to handle the personal data, it is not “provision” to a third party. However, the business must itself take security measures, and for retained personal data, must make known to the person the names of the foreign country where the provider is located and the foreign country where the servers are located, and the measures taken after understanding the systems of those countries (understanding the external environment). [J6]
- A business handling personal information (Article 16(2)) is one that uses a personal information database or the like (systematically organized so that specific personal information can be searched by computer; Article 16(1)) for its business. The Personal Information Protection Commission's guidelines (general rules) 2-4 give “an e-mail address book stored in e-mail software (where information combining e-mail addresses and names is entered)” as an example of a personal information database. [J6] [J15]
- A business handling personal information specifies the purpose of use as far as possible (Article 17), and upon acquisition promptly notifies or publishes it unless published in advance (Article 21(1)). For retained personal data, it makes ① its name and address (for a corporation, the name of its representative), ② the purposes of use of all retained personal data, ③ the procedure for responding to requests for disclosure and the like, and ④ matters specified by Cabinet Order (Enforcement Order Article 10: security measures taken, where to make complaints, the name of the accredited personal information protection organization) knowable to the person (including responding without delay on the person's request) (Article 32(1)). [J15]
- Consequences for the design (Edition 2):
  - NRSD does not receive Users' C2PA signing information, photos, or records (Design Plan D-5-1“The System does not confirm Users and collects no User information. No registration is required in order to use the System”). No User information is placed in the Public Repository either (Basic Design Chapter 8, 3 “Public Repository”).
  - All NRSD receives is feedback e-mail Users send themselves (Basic Design Chapter 10, 9 “Feedback Channel”). The mailbox can be searched, so it may constitute a personal information database. ### L-21“Copying for evidence (Japan, China, US)” Japan: Copying in judicial proceedings (preserving evidence)

- Copyright Act Article 41-2(1): “A work may be reproduced where it is deemed necessary for judicial proceedings and administrative adjudication proceedings, to the extent deemed necessary.” However, excluding where it unreasonably prejudices the copyright holder's interests in light of the type and use of the work and the number and manner of copies. [J1]
- Consequences for the design:
  - Most evidence consists of reposts of the User's own works (the User is the rights holder), and copying poses no problem.
  - Third parties' works (other images and text) captured in screen records of reposted pages may be treated as copying to the extent necessary for judicial proceedings.   - The danger of saving pages containing illegal content (obscene material, etc.) is explained to Users (caution on screen in Chapter 6 “Registration and Evidence Preservation”).
- United States: Bond v. Blum, 317 F.3d 385 (4th Cir. 2003) found copying a work as evidence in litigation to be fair use as used for its content rather than its expression (though it is a judgment by the factors of Section 107, not a uniform rule). [U8]
- China: Copyright Law Article 24 limits fair use (合理使用) to 12 enumerated categories and “other cases provided by laws and administrative regulations”. No category explicitly covering private persons copying others' works as evidence for litigation could be confirmed in public information (there is a category for official duties of state organs). [C5] Therefore the treatment of third parties' works captured requires confirmation by experts (kept as unresolved).

### L-24“Treatment of electronic signatures and electronic data as evidence (Japan, China, US)” Evidential treatment of electronic signatures and electronic data

- Japan: Electronic Signatures Act Article 3 presumes an electromagnetic record to be authentically made when “an electronic signature by the principal (limited to one that only the principal can perform by properly managing the codes and articles necessary to perform it)” has been made. [J9]
- China: Electronic Signature Law Article 13 defines as a “reliable electronic signature” one that meets all of: ① the signature creation data is exclusively owned by the signatory, ② at the time of signing, only the signatory controls the creation data, ③ any alteration of the signature after signing can be detected, ④ any alteration of the content and form after signing can be detected; Article 14 gives it the same effect as a handwritten signature or seal. [C7]
- China: Provisions on Evidence in Civil Procedures (2019 amendment) Article 93 determines the authenticity of electronic data comprehensively from the integrity and reliability of the systems for its generation, storage, and transmission, and Article 94 allows the authenticity of electronic data provided or confirmed by a neutral third-party platform that records and stores it to be confirmed unless there is contrary evidence. [C8]
- United States: Federal Rule of Evidence 902(14) allows data copied from electronic devices, storage media, or files, verified by a process of digital identification (usually matching hash values), to be self-authenticating (proving authenticity without a witness) by a certification of a qualified person. [U10]
- Consequences for the design:
  - The System's signing keys are kept in the OS keystore and leave the device only in backup files encrypted with the User's passphrase (on Linux without Secret Service, in a key file encrypted with a passphrase; Basic Design Chapter 2, 7 “Signing Keys”, Chapter 8, 5 “Backup Files”). The keys can be used by the device's user and by those who have the backup file and its passphrase. This design aims to follow ① (creation data exclusively owned by the signatory) and ② (only the signatory controls it at signing) of China's Article 13, and “only the principal can perform” of Japan's Article 3, but management of backup files and passphrases is left to the User (requires confirmation by experts). However, the certificate is from the User's own certificate authority made on the device (Basic Design Chapter 2, 3 “Certificates”); no third party has verified the principal and it does not prove a link to the real name, so it cannot be said that the presumption of Japan's Article 3 can be claimed as a matter of course (requires confirmation by experts).
  - Timestamps come from external TSAs (trusted timestamps in China). They may count as confirmation by a “neutral third party” under China's Article 94.
  - The exported evidence package includes a list of SHA-256 of all files (Basic Design Chapter 6 “Registration and Evidence Preservation”). It is material from which a qualified person can make the certification of U.S. 902(14).
- External means of reinforcing evidence (those for which Design Plan 10.3 sets references)
  - China: the Provisions of the Supreme People's Court on Several Issues Concerning the Trial of Cases by Internet Courts (in force September 7, 2018) Article 11 provides that internet courts confirm electronic data submitted by parties whose authenticity can be proven by technical means of collection, fixing, and tamper prevention such as electronic signatures, trusted timestamps, hash value verification, and blockchain, or by certification from electronic evidence-collection and preservation platforms. [C14]
  - Japan: notarial deeds of factual experiments, in which a notary records facts directly experienced with the five senses, have their originals kept at the notary office and are used to preserve evidence such as the display state of web pages. Fees are determined by time and workload. [J26]
  - Consequences for the design: the System does not perform these procedures. The export of the evidence package (Basic Design Chapter 6, 3.4 “Export”) is in a file form that can be brought as is into these procedures, and names and links are shown as guidance (Chapter 6, 3.2 “Trusted timestamps”).

### L-34“Third-party personal information in evidence (Japan)” Japan: Protection of personal information (records on the User's device)
